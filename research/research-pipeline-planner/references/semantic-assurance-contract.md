# Semantic Assurance and Real-Project Regression Contract

Use this contract after the PR 11-14 structural authorities exist and before claiming that the suite materially reduces research oscillation, literature omission, or selective evidence promotion.

The contract is suite-level. It reads the existing canonical commitment, literature, novelty, experiment, result-audit, and paper-binding authorities. It adds one commitment-transition ledger because a transition cannot be validated from one current commitment file. It does not introduce a second evidence graph or a second literature frontier.

## Defensible validation sequence

### 1. Freeze the evaluation protocol before implementation

Before changing the semantic controls:

- freeze the expected D0-D4 labels for the historical project transitions;
- separate implementation examples from locked retrospective regression cases;
- place hidden literature challenges under separate custody where feasible;
- define permitted run-exclusion classes before inspecting scientific outcomes;
- define route-level acceptance checks and bounded failure language.

This step prevents the implementation from being tuned until already-known cases pass without a stable decision rule.

### 2. Develop PR 15 with realistic cases in the loop

Realistic project histories and held-out literature challenges are developmental evidence during PR 15, not work postponed wholesale until after merge. They should expose missing semantic controls while the implementation can still change.

Developmental use does not authorize a general effectiveness claim. Repeatedly inspecting a case while implementing against it makes that case a development fixture, even when it came from a real project.

### 3. Run a locked integrated merge gate

After implementation is frozen, run:

- all PR 11-14 structural and reciprocal-link tests;
- commitment-transition regressions;
- literature perspective, seed-class, saturation, challenge-recall, and novelty-artifact regressions;
- adverse-run accounting, source-derived assurance, and structured-scope regressions;
- locked Normality Milieu and LLM Triangulation transition fixtures;
- at least three literature challenge domains;
- a mixed positive, negative, null, and technical-failure evidence route.

A passing merge gate establishes that the committed implementation handles the declared retrospective and held-out cases. It does not establish general prospective effectiveness.

### 4. Validate prospectively after merge

Use new projects, new literature topics, and reviewers who did not tune the implementation to estimate:

- major identity-change detection and false-positive rates;
- critical-paper recall and importance-weighted recall;
- late conclusion-changing omissions;
- valid adverse-evidence retention;
- time to execute, submit, split, park, or kill;
- whether projects reopen ideation less often without evidential triggers.

Only this prospective stage can support a broader claim that the suite improves research outcomes beyond the locked regression set.

## Commitment transition authority

Canonical additional artifact:

```text
commitment-transitions.json
```

The artifact contains the canonical sequence of commitment snapshots and transition records in one authority. Every adjacent snapshot pair has exactly one transition record, and the latest snapshot must match the current `research-commitment.json`.

A transition records:

- stable transition and snapshot IDs;
- exact canonical before and after SHA-256 digests;
- digest-bound identity anchors for the question, central object, and evidence obligation;
- the exact deterministic field diff;
- declared and computed D0-D4 classes;
- from and to identity versions;
- the exact predecessor pivot trigger or kill condition, by kind, index, and text;
- one-use authorization bound to the transition ID and both digests;
- switching cost, discarded evidence, and successor assessment;
- old-lineage closure bound to the exact predecessor digest and identity version for D4.

Deterministic drift floors:

- paper ID replacement is D4;
- contribution-class replacement, or replacement of a digest-bound question, central-object, or evidence-obligation anchor, is at least D3;
- wording changes to an identity field remain D0 only when its stable anchor ID and canonical definition digest remain unchanged;
- minimum-claim or mandatory-evidence narrowing that preserves the identity is at least D2;
- other substantive control changes are at least D1;
- no substantive change is D0.

A declared class weaker than the computed floor fails. D0-D2 preserve the identity version. D3 increments it by one. D4 creates a new paper ID at version 1 and closes the predecessor lineage. An authorization may be consumed once only.

## Retrieval-recall and novelty controls

The corpus manifest remains the literature authority. Its `semantic_assurance` object records:

- a decision for every required coverage perspective;
- reciprocal coverage-question IDs;
- seed classes;
- challenge-evaluation digest and mode;
- saturation evidence;
- criticality downgrade decisions;
- any narrow-topic exception and its scope limit.

Systematic or strong-novelty routes declare decisions for:

- direct method or phenomenon;
- strongest competing formulation;
- negative or null evidence;
- foundational ancestry;
- recent frontier;
- methodological validity;
- benchmark or dataset lineage;
- reproducibility or implementation;
- venue and author/lab clusters.

Strong novelty normally requires foundational, closest-recent, and competing-or-critical seed classes, at least three seeds and corpus records, and either a withheld challenge set or materially independent search review. A withheld challenge is frozen before search, held by a named custodian, digest-bound, disjoint from visible seeds, and represented in the frozen corpus after recovery. A narrow-topic exception must state its rationale, exact scope limit, and materially independent review basis.

A saturated question requires at least two distinct reciprocal search rounds with non-increasing yield and at least one strict decline, or a hard source boundary plus a disproportionality rationale.

Ratings 4-5 additionally require:

- a substantive nearest-work matrix;
- a high, critical, or kill-shot overlap comparison;
- substantive novelty search or reciprocal delegation rows;
- representation of every high-priority novelty-critical question;
- at least one kill-shot objection;
- an explicit condition that would lower the rating;
- no unresolved material missing-prior-work entry.

## Evidence selection and assurance derivation

`results-audit.json` remains the result authority. The semantic extension adds:

- a structured scope registry;
- frozen eligibility rules whose exact digests are bound into source runs and work items;
- source-run scope, eligibility, technical-validity, and outcome classes;
- controlled technical exclusion classes;
- adverse-evidence dispositions;
- source conditions used to derive the maximum assurance class.

Allowed exclusion classes are limited to:

- corrupted input;
- executor failure before measurement;
- protocol-ineligible configuration;
- duplicate logical attempt;
- invalid hidden-truth boundary.

A valid positive, negative, null, or contradictory outcome is not an exclusion. Same-scope adverse outcomes remain in the audit and receive an explicit manuscript consequence.

The maximum assurance class is derived from the exact experiment block, frozen work item, and source run. Confirmatory support requires both block and work item to be confirmatory or stronger, substantive block selection and outcome-accounting contracts, a committed or executing paper at start, a frozen gate, the claim and decision rule frozen before outcome inspection, complete outcome accounting, no material deviation, and no disallowed hidden-truth access. An exploratory source cannot be promoted by filling confirmatory audit fields.

## Structured scopes

Both result audits and paper bindings reference structured scope objects. Every dimension carries a stable semantic ID plus a human-readable description for population, environment or evaluator, intervention, comparator, outcomes, time or version, and exclusions. Scope identity is computed from those stable dimension IDs rather than free-text descriptions.

Rephrasing a description therefore cannot manufacture a new scope. Semantically identical objects cannot use different scope IDs, an asserted empirical claim uses the exact same scope identity as its positive audit, and an audit exclusion identifies the exact changed dimensions rather than asserting a difference in prose.

## Real-project fixtures

The repository regression index includes:

- Normality Milieu transitions, including a D3 formal-route reconfiguration and a D2 narrowing;
- LLM Triangulation D1 implementation repairs, D2 claim narrowing, and a D4 successor result-bearing paper;
- at least three literature challenge domains;
- an evidence route with positive, negative, null, and technical-failure outcomes.

These are regression cases, not proof that future projects will behave similarly.

## Bounded conclusion

A passing semantic-assurance route establishes repository-local consistency of the declared transition history, retrieval controls, challenge accounting, novelty artifacts, run eligibility, source-derived assurance, structured scope, and locked regression fixtures.

It does not establish literature completeness, scientific novelty, semantic correctness, authenticated execution, external immutability, independent replication, or prospective research effectiveness.
