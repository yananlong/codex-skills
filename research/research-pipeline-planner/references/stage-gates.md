# Research Stage Gates

Use these gates to decide whether to proceed, revise scope, or stop.

## Evidence-promotion gate

Apply this gate whenever a workflow tries to promote an artifact or result beyond exploratory status. Use `epistemic-assurance-contract.md` for the full contract.

- Proceed when the evidence class is explicit, the promotion record is complete, material predecessor failures have evidence-backed dispositions, and the requested level of independence is real rather than nominal.
- Revise or narrow when the work is useful but remains exploratory, the decision or loss contract is incomplete, the case selection is outcome-informed, the non-vacuity check is missing, or independence is only self-review.
- Stop the promotion when confirmatory or high-stakes advancement depends on self-attested controls, hidden-truth leakage, omitted failure states, a vacuous comparator, or an unresolved material predecessor failure.
- Do not stop useful exploration merely because stronger assurance is unavailable. Reclassify the evidence and narrow the claim instead.

## Literature review gate

- Proceed when the core question is specific enough to search.
- Revise when the topic is broad enough that search terms collapse into noise.
- Stop when the domain or research question is still missing.

## Ideation gate

- Proceed when the direction, constraints, and contribution type are specific enough to produce testable candidate ideas.
- Revise when candidates are mostly slogans, obvious applications, or ungrounded combinations.
- Stop when no surviving idea has a concrete hypothesis, plausible novelty signal, and minimum validation path.

## Novelty review gate

- Proceed when the proposed method or finding is concrete enough to compare.
- Revise when the idea is still rhetorical or bundles too many moving parts.
- Stop when search access or a corpus is unavailable.
- Treat the novelty decision as positioning evidence, not as evidence that the proposed method works or that an experiment route is valid.

## Experiment plan gate

- Proceed when the claim and decision rule are explicit.
- Revise when the plan still mixes multiple claims or confounded interventions.
- Stop when the evaluation target is undefined.
- For confirmatory plans, also require a complete loss or outcome contract, explicit case-selection rule, non-vacuity preflight, failure inheritance, and an honest independence statement.

## Paper review gate

- Proceed when there is a concrete paper artifact or URL worth scrutinizing closely.
- Revise when the draft is so incomplete that section-level critique would mostly produce noise.
- Stop when the requested review would require guessing missing paper content or fabricating evidence.

## Results audit gate

- Proceed when there is a real result artifact to inspect.
- Revise when the claimed conclusion is broader than the measured evidence.
- Stop when the headline numbers cannot be traced to source artifacts.
- Do not issue an unqualified pass from file presence, validator success, agreement among shared implementations, or self-attested controls alone.

## Review loop gate

- Proceed when the artifact is important enough to benefit from iterative review.
- Revise when the artifact is too incomplete to support adversarial feedback.
- Stop when there is no concrete artifact to review.
- Carry material predecessor failures forward until new evidence resolves them; reclassification or omission does not close an issue.

## Paper plan gate

- Proceed when there is enough supported evidence to build a coherent paper story.
- Revise when claims still outrun the evidence.
- Stop when the user is asking for manuscript structure without any credible support.

## Rebuttal gate

- Proceed when there are concrete reviews or decision-relevant committee comments to answer.
- Revise when the venue route, policy constraints, or supporting evidence are still too unclear to draft safely.
- Stop when the response would require fabricated results, invented manuscript changes, or guessed reviewer positions.
