# Research Stage Gates

Use these gates to decide whether to proceed, revise scope, request a pivot, park a successor idea, or stop. In an orchestrated paper-bearing project, read `research-commitment.json` and `research-commitment-contract.md` before authorizing any transition.

## Paper-identity gate

Apply this gate before every consequential cross-stage transition.

- Proceed when the work preserves the committed main question, central object or phenomenon, contribution class, minimum publishable claim, and primary evidence obligation, or when the change is an explicitly recorded D0-D2 refinement.
- Revise when the proposed work is useful but does not produce the declared `next_mandatory_evidence_artifact`.
- Request a pivot for D3 changes: contribution reconfiguration, central-object replacement, primary-evidence replacement, or route conversion.
- Close and create a successor lineage for D4 changes: a genuinely different paper identity.
- Park attractive alternatives by default after commitment. A downstream worker may recommend a pivot but cannot enact D3-D4 changes.
- Do not treat reframing, renaming, deleting a claim, or changing contribution class as resolution of predecessor failures.

## Evidence-promotion gate

Apply this gate whenever a workflow tries to promote an artifact or result beyond exploratory status. Use `epistemic-assurance-contract.md` for the full contract.

- Proceed when the evidence class is explicit, the promotion record is complete, material predecessor failures have evidence-backed dispositions, and the requested level of independence is real rather than nominal.
- Revise or narrow when the work is useful but remains exploratory, the decision or loss contract is incomplete, the case selection is outcome-informed, the non-vacuity check is missing, or independence is only self-review.
- Stop the promotion when confirmatory or high-stakes advancement depends on self-attested controls, hidden-truth leakage, omitted failure states, a vacuous comparator, or an unresolved material predecessor failure.
- Do not stop useful exploration merely because stronger assurance is unavailable. Reclassify the evidence and narrow the claim instead.

## Literature review gate

- Proceed when the review profile, question, scope boundaries, source plan, and expected assurance level are explicit.
- For `comprehensive-systematic` and `bounded-systematic`, also require a visible seed set, citation-search decisions, recall audit, corpus manifest, and stopping rationale before expensive extraction and synthesis.
- Revise when the topic is broad enough that search terms collapse into noise, known relevant seeds are missed, citation searching yields material additions that expose a deficient original strategy, or important coverage cells remain unexplained.
- Stop comprehensive claims when retrieval assurance is insufficient. Continue as a bounded review, critical evidence map, rapid scan, or novelty-oriented prior-art search with an honest label.
- Never infer adequate recall from PRISMA counts, canonical URLs, file presence, or validator success alone.

## Ideation gate

- Proceed freely while the commitment status is `exploring` and the direction, constraints, and contribution type are specific enough to produce testable candidates.
- After commitment, route new ideas to the successor parking policy unless a recorded pivot trigger or kill condition has fired.
- Revise when candidates are mostly slogans, obvious applications, ungrounded combinations, or replacements for the active paper generated merely to escape a local objection.
- Stop when no surviving idea has a concrete hypothesis, plausible novelty signal, minimum validation path, and credible route to being wrong.

## Novelty review gate

- Proceed when the proposed method or finding is concrete enough to compare and literature assurance is proportionate to the strength of the novelty claim.
- Revise when the idea is rhetorical, bundles too many moving parts, or depends on incomplete prior-art coverage.
- Stop strong priority or first-of-kind language when recall assurance is inadequate.
- Treat the novelty decision as positioning evidence, not evidence that the method works or that an experiment route is valid.
- A novelty review may recommend `continue`, `narrow`, `pivot-request`, `kill`, or `park-successor`; it may not silently rewrite the commitment contract.

## Experiment plan gate

- Proceed when the committed claim and decision rule are explicit and the plan targets the declared primary evidence obligation.
- Revise when the plan mixes multiple claims, changes the central object, or adds optional experiments before the next mandatory evidence artifact.
- Stop when the evaluation target is undefined or the plan requires an unapproved D3-D4 change.
- For confirmatory plans, also require a complete loss or outcome contract, explicit case-selection rule, non-vacuity preflight, failure inheritance, and an honest independence statement.

## Paper review gate

- Proceed when there is a concrete paper artifact or URL worth scrutinizing closely.
- Revise when the draft is so incomplete that section-level critique would mostly produce noise.
- Stop when the requested review would require guessing missing paper content or fabricating evidence.
- Record whether proposed fixes are D0-D2 repairs or D3-D4 pivot requests.

## Results audit gate

- Proceed when there is a real result artifact to inspect and it corresponds to the declared next mandatory evidence artifact.
- Revise when the claimed conclusion is broader than the measured evidence.
- Stop when headline numbers cannot be traced to source artifacts.
- Do not issue an unqualified pass from file presence, validator success, agreement among shared implementations, or self-attested controls alone.

## Review loop gate

- Proceed when the artifact is important enough to benefit from iterative review.
- Revise when the artifact is too incomplete to support adversarial feedback.
- Stop when there is no concrete artifact to review.
- Carry material predecessor failures forward until new evidence resolves them; reclassification or omission does not close an issue.
- Every round must classify identity drift as D0-D4. D3-D4 findings become pivot requests rather than ordinary issue closure.

## Paper plan gate

- Proceed when supported evidence can sustain the committed minimum publishable claim.
- Revise when claims outrun evidence or the plan prioritizes optional extensions over the minimum sufficient paper.
- Stop when there is no credible support for the main claim or when the outline changes the paper identity without authorization.
- Mark every section and exhibit as `submission-critical`, `defensive`, or `optional`.

## Rebuttal gate

- Proceed when there are concrete reviews or decision-relevant committee comments to answer.
- Revise when the venue route, policy constraints, or supporting evidence remain unclear.
- Stop when the response would require fabricated results, invented manuscript changes, guessed reviewer positions, or an unapproved paper-identity change.
