# Market Diligence Guide

Use this guide to search for market, competitor, buyer, procurement, regulatory, reimbursement, standards, and funding signals.

## Market map construction

For each candidate segment, map:

| Element | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| user | product docs, workflow descriptions, job roles, customer stories |
| economic buyer | pricing pages, public filings, procurement notices, reimbursement owner, budget owner |
| budget proxy | comparable purchases, public spend, listed pricing, labor cost, instrument cost, claims cost, grant size |
| current workaround | customer forums, manuals, job postings, service providers, incumbent features |
| competitors | product pages, filings, patents, awards, case studies, procurement incumbents |
| substitutes | internal teams, services, manual workflow, cheaper adjacent tools, regulatory avoidance |
| adoption blocker | integrations, validation burden, security/privacy, regulatory clearance, standards, retraining |
| proof needed | procurement criteria, clinical endpoint, benchmark, qualification test, ROI model, compliance review |

## Competitor and substitute mapping

Classify each alternative as one of:

- direct product competitor
- platform incumbent
- component supplier
- service provider
- internal build/workaround
- adjacent substitute
- research prototype
- open-source or free alternative
- regulatory/procurement workaround

Do not call a company a competitor merely because it has a patent. Require evidence of product, service, roadmap, published research program, procurement activity, or plausible integration path.

## Public-company diligence

Use public filings for segment revenue, stated risk factors, product strategy, customer concentration, R&D direction, acquisition history, and regulatory exposure. Extract exact filing type and date. Avoid implying a specific product's revenue if the filing only reports an aggregated segment.

## Procurement and public-sector diligence

Use procurement notices and award data to identify buyer language, NAICS/PSC codes, incumbent vendors, contract sizes, agency mission needs, evaluation criteria, and buying cycles. A notice is evidence of a purchasing process, not proof of a broad commercial market.

## Regulatory, reimbursement, and standards diligence

Map whether adoption requires clearance, approval, certification, reimbursement, coding, safety testing, privacy/security review, environmental permitting, or standards conformance. Distinguish permission to sell from payment, procurement, and workflow adoption.

## Funding and translational-program diligence

Map non-dilutive funding and translational programs only if they change the next decision. Record eligibility, entity type, deadlines/cycles, required matching funds, award size if sourced, and the risk the funding would retire. Funding interest is not customer demand unless paired with post-funding procurement or adoption evidence.

## Search query patterns

- `"<technology>" "<application>" product pricing`
- `"<workflow problem>" RFP OR procurement OR solicitation`
- `"<technology>" "FDA" OR "510(k)" OR "de novo" OR "PMA"`
- `"<technology>" reimbursement OR CPT OR HCPCS OR coverage`
- `"<incumbent>" 10-K risk factors <technology>`
- `"<agency/customer>" "<problem>" "award" OR "contract"`
- `"<technology>" "SBIR" OR "STTR" OR "grant" OR "award"`
- `"<standard>" "<technology>" certification OR compliance`

## Decision interpretation

- Strongest evidence: named buyer paid or procured, public purchase/award, customer-visible pricing, regulatory path already used by comparables, high-fit incumbent roadmap, or repeat purchasing behavior.
- Medium evidence: funded pilots with named stakeholders, serious RFP language, public-company strategic priority, customer case studies, credible analyst/trade data.
- Weak evidence: broad market-size claims, press coverage, unsourced forecasts, patents without products, academic citations, accelerator/grant interest without buyer follow-through.
