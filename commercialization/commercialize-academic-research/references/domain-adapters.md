# Domain Adapters

Use this file only after the generic commercialization chain is clear. Apply the adapter checks that could change the decision; do not paste the whole checklist into the answer.

## Generic adapter pattern

For the chosen domain, identify the buyer-owned metric, current workaround, proof artifact required before purchase or partnership, blocking adoption issue, and cheapest invalidating test.

## Health, biomed, diagnostics, and clinical AI

- Map user, patient or beneficiary, clinician/operator, economic buyer, payer or reimbursement owner, privacy/security gate, regulator, and procurement committee.
- Identify regulatory pathway, clinical evidence burden, IRB or ethics needs, privacy/security obligations, liability exposure, and post-market monitoring.
- Check reimbursement, coding, site-of-care economics, workflow integration, and who benefits financially.
- Treat retrospective performance, mouse data, benchmark AUROC, or biological plausibility as technical evidence, not buying evidence.
- Require a pilot gate with patient/data access, clinical workflow owner, success metric, compliance path, and adoption decision.

## AI, software, data, and evaluation assets

- Separate model or algorithm quality from deployability: data access, integration, latency, reliability, monitoring, security review, privacy, and support burden.
- Check benchmark relevance to the buyer-owned workflow metric, not just academic SOTA.
- Identify data rights, open-source licenses, model weights/API dependencies, customer data retention, and redistribution constraints.
- Compare product sale against component/API, internal tooling, data/evaluation asset, services, and licensing routes.
- Validate willingness to pay with a current budget, avoided labor/risk, revenue lift, or compliance obligation.

## Climate, energy, infrastructure, and industrial decarbonization

- Use TRL/MRL/ARL together: technical performance, manufacturing or deployment readiness, and adoption risks can block commercialization independently.
- Check unit economics, energy/material balance, capex/opex, supply chain, permitting, interconnection, safety, siting, offtake, warranty, and bankability.
- Identify whether the first customer is an end user, project developer, EPC, utility, industrial operator, OEM, or government buyer.
- Treat policy incentives and grants as accelerants, not substitutes for customer pull, unless the near-term path is explicitly funding-first translation.
- Prefer partnerships when demonstration sites, regulatory access, balance sheet, or channel control matter more than standalone product speed.

## Materials, chemistry, manufacturing, and hardtech components

- Check manufacturability, yield, quality control, durability, reliability, environmental health and safety, scale-up, and supplier qualification.
- Identify the qualification protocol customers already use and whether lab tests predict field performance.
- Map component buyer, spec owner, manufacturing owner, quality/regulatory owner, and channel partner separately.
- Compare licensing, OEM/component sale, joint development, and service-enabled testing before recommending a venture-backed startup.
- Use a milestone sequence such as lab result -> prototype batch -> qualification test -> paid JDA -> production-intent pilot.

## Robotics, instruments, sensors, and deployed hardware

- Check field reliability, uptime, maintenance, calibration, installation, training, safety certification, warranty, consumables, and support economics.
- Identify whether the customer buys hardware, a managed service, per-use throughput, data, or a component integrated by an OEM.
- Require evidence from the target operating environment; lab demos and benchmark scenes are not enough.
- Compare capex sale, lease, robotics-as-a-service, OEM partnership, and component licensing.
- Include deployment-owner and operator incentives, not only executive buyer value.

## Public sector, education, and social-impact deployments

- Map procurement authority, budget cycle, fiscal sustainability, privacy/security review, implementation owner, frontline user, and affected community.
- Check whether outcomes can be measured within a decision cycle and whether the implementing institution has operational capacity.
- Treat grant-funded pilots as adoption evidence only when there is a post-grant budget or procurement path.
- Identify legal, accessibility, equity, data governance, and stakeholder legitimacy risks early.
- Prefer staged pilots with explicit continuation criteria over broad platform claims.

## Funding-first translational path checks

Use this route when the asset is promising but not commercially ready enough for customers, licensees, or investors.

- Specify the non-dilutive or translational funding target, eligibility, deadline or cycle, required entity type, and matching-fund obligations.
- Define which risk the funding retires: technical proof, manufacturability, clinical/regulatory evidence, customer discovery, prototype hardening, or partner access.
- Require a post-funding commercialization gate; do not let funding become the goal.
- Compare funding-first translation against licensing, sponsored research, JDA, and startup paths.
